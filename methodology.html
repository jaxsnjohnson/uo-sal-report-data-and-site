How to read the data

Keep the report, the period, and the pay measure in view.

Report periods and pay measures

The University of Oregon’s Office of Data Enablement lists classified and unclassified reports in two series. UO says fall census salaries reflect November 1 and are published in January; fiscal-year actual pay covers July 1–June 30 and is published in September.

SeriesWhat it describesHow to compare it
Fall censusA fall snapshot (October 31 in 2014; November 1 thereafter)Compare rates with the same stated units and appointment basis.
Fiscal year (2020–21 onward)Actual pay during July 1–June 30Compare fiscal years while accounting for partial-year work and report coverage.
Earlier personnel listsHistorical snapshot or period rates, including FY 2014–15 to 2018–19Use the printed period and appointment term. These older FY lists do not report actual earnings.

A salary rate does not establish annual earnings, take-home pay, benefits, or total compensation.

Original amounts stay in their original units.

UO defines its salary rate as the full-time amount for an entire term of service. The imported 12-month rates and 9-month academic-year rates are separate measures. The explorer does not multiply rates by 12 or 2,080 or apply FTE automatically. Each imported report requires a reviewed definition of its amount column. Original values remain visible beside the source PDF page. Appointment percent is divided by 100 to obtain FTE; actual-pay reports do not supply this field, so their FTE stays unavailable.

Unavailable amounts are excluded from medians. A zero or negative rate is flagged for review. A fiscal-year zero or negative amount is preserved as reported and included in actual-pay statistics, because actual pay can include adjustments. No value is silently replaced with an invented salary.

Report rows are not a verified employee headcount.

A person may have more than one appointment or appear in more than one report. Dashboard counts describe distinct exact names with matching entries. Cards, classification splits, and rankings use the latest matching entry per name. Medians use the usable amounts on those representative entries only after one pay measure is selected. Same-date ties use source row number, then record ID; the representative entry is not a total across jobs. These results may span different years and do not describe a current workforce. Multiple appointments are not automatically summed. Departments and job titles are used as reported.

Classified and unclassified are UO’s report categories. They do not independently establish an individual’s union membership, bargaining unit, or supervisory status. Appearance in a report is evidence for its stated date or period, not proof of current employment.

Search across all reports, grouped by exact name.

One result collects entries with the exact same printed name across the full archive. Expanding a result shows all those entries, even when a period, department, or pay filter matched only some of them. Every entry retains its date, source PDF page, and pay units.

This grouping is not a verified identity link: identical names can refer to different people, while name changes and spelling variants remain separate groups. Accent-insensitive search can find variants without merging them. The importer preserves each source row and requires documented review for person-identity links. Missing observations do not establish unemployment.

Charts have a defined population.

The dashboard, classification split, and department and job-title rankings follow the period, measure, and search filters, using the latest matching entry per exact name. Historical comparisons use all imported reports for the selected series and measure, independent of the search query. Their cross-sections can change because of coverage, staffing mix, and appointment patterns.

Classified and unclassified reports keep their own dates. Reports with different pay measures are never combined into one median or trend line. No inflation adjustment, collective bargaining increase, or peer comparison is inferred from the OSU project.

The source chain is inspectable.

The source archive distinguishes links listed by UO, PDFs preserved locally, and reports imported into the explorer. Saved PDFs carry SHA-256 checksums; reviewed transcriptions record source pages and row numbers. Catalog capture dates remain separate from report dates.

The supplied SharePoint archive contains 93 PDFs and 353,333 job records, covering periods from June 2009 through June 2026. Automated extraction checks reconcile personnel blocks and amounts on every page; representative layouts were visually reviewed. Individual records have not all been manually checked. Original fields and source locations are retained.

The supplied archive has no FY 2019–20 reports and lacks an unclassified counterpart for the period ending May 31, 2012. Missing reports do not establish zero pay or unemployment. Repeated records, zero amounts, and negative actual-pay adjustments are retained as printed.
