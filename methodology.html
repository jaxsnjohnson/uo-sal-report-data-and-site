How to read the data

Keep the report, the period, and the pay measure in view.

Two report series. Two different questions.

The University of Oregon’s Office of Data Enablement lists classified and unclassified reports in two series. UO says fall census salaries reflect November 1 and are published in January; fiscal-year actual pay covers July 1–June 30 and is published in September.

SeriesWhat it describesHow to compare it
Fall censusA salary snapshot at November 1Compare rates with the same stated units and appointment basis.
Fiscal yearActual pay during July 1–June 30Compare fiscal years while accounting for partial-year work and report coverage.

A salary rate does not establish annual earnings, take-home pay, benefits, or total compensation.

Original amounts stay in their original units.

Annual, academic-year, monthly, and hourly rates are separate measures. The explorer does not multiply rates by 12 or 2,080 or apply FTE automatically. Each imported report requires a reviewed definition of its amount column. Original values remain visible beside the source PDF page.

Unavailable amounts are excluded from medians. A zero or negative rate is flagged for review. A fiscal-year zero or negative amount is preserved as reported and included in actual-pay statistics, because actual pay can include adjustments. No value is silently replaced with an invented salary.

Report rows are not a verified employee headcount.

A person may have more than one appointment or appear in more than one report. Dashboard counts describe source rows, and medians describe usable amounts on those rows. Multiple appointments are not automatically summed. Departments and job titles are used as reported.

Classified and unclassified are UO’s report categories. They do not independently establish an individual’s union membership, bargaining unit, or supervisory status. Appearance in a report is evidence for its stated date or period, not proof of current employment.

Histories require reviewed identity links.

The importer never joins people just because their names match. Records remain separate until an explicit linkage has been checked and documented. Name changes can leave histories incomplete; identical names can refer to different people. Missing observations do not establish unemployment.

When linked observations have the same pay measure, the explorer can draw their reported amounts over time. Multiple appointments on the same date stay in the table rather than being added into an unsupported total. Missing amounts appear as gaps.

Charts have a defined population.

The dashboard, classification split, and department and job-title rankings follow the current period, measure, and search filters. Historical comparisons use all imported reports for the selected measure, independent of the search query. Their cross-sections can change because of coverage, staffing mix, and appointment patterns.

Classified and unclassified reports keep their own dates. Reports with different pay measures are never combined into one median or trend line. No inflation adjustment, collective bargaining increase, or peer comparison is inferred from the OSU project.

The source chain is inspectable.

The source archive distinguishes links listed by UO, PDFs preserved locally, and reports imported into the explorer. Saved PDFs carry SHA-256 checksums; reviewed transcriptions record source pages and row numbers. Catalog capture dates remain separate from report dates.

The repository currently supports an explicit import of reviewed observations. UO’s PDF layout must be inspected before adding an automated parser. A report is not shown as imported simply because its download link is known.

An independent public-data project.

This site adapts the dark, card-based design and static architecture of the OSU Salary Transparency explorer. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the University of Oregon. Verify figures against the linked original reports.

Search runs in your browser. This repository does not include analytics or send search queries to a server. Opening an official report takes you to UO or its SharePoint service.
